# Why "Only 3 Left" Works on Your Brain

## Hook

Pip is scrolling a shopping app late at night. He isn't looking for anything in particular.

Then a pair of headphones appears, with a small red banner underneath. Only three left in stock. Two other people are viewing this item.

Pip didn't want headphones a minute ago. Ninety seconds later, he has bought them.

Nothing about the headphones changed. Only the number did. In this video, you'll learn why scarcity takes over your brain, and how to stop it from spending your money for you.

## Less feels like more

Here's a simple experiment that has been run in many forms.

People are given cookies from one of two jars. One jar is full. The other jar holds only two cookies. The cookies are exactly the same.

Yet people rate the cookies from the nearly empty jar as tastier and more valuable.

And it gets stronger. When a jar starts full and then has cookies taken away in front of them, people value those cookies even more. Something that is becoming rare feels more precious than something that was always rare.

## Why your brain falls for it

There are two reasons this works so well.

The first is a shortcut. For most of history, things that were hard to get really were more valuable. So your brain learned a rule. If it's rare, it must be good. Most of the time the rule works, which is exactly why it's so easy to exploit.

The second reason is freedom. When something becomes harder to get, it feels like a choice is being taken away from you. And people hate losing choices. So you want the thing more, not because you need it, but because you might lose the chance to have it.

## Where you see it every day

Once you know this, you start seeing it everywhere.

A countdown timer on a checkout page. A sale that ends at midnight. A limited edition, a members only offer, a deal for the first hundred customers.

Some of these are real. Many are not. The timer resets when you refresh the page. The last three items are still the last three items next week.

The goal is always the same. To make you decide fast, before the thinking part of your brain catches up.

## How to protect yourself

So how do you stay in control?

The first move is to notice the feeling. That sudden rush to act now is the signal. When you feel it, slow down on purpose.

The second move is to separate two questions. Whether it's rare, and whether you actually want it. Scarcity only answers the first one.

The third move is to imagine it is not scarce. Picture a full shelf of the same item, available any time. Ask yourself whether you would still buy it at this price. If not, the number was doing the selling, not the product.

And the fourth move is to give yourself a rule. Anything over a set amount waits twenty four hours. Real value survives a day. Pressure usually doesn't.

## Close

Let's go back to Pip.

The next time a red banner appears, he notices the rush, and he closes the app. He imagines a full shelf. The next morning, he doesn't even remember the headphones.

So here's what to remember. Rare things feel more valuable, even when they aren't. Things that are becoming rare feel even more valuable. And the best defence is a pause, because pressure fades and real value doesn't.
