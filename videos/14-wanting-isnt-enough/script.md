# Why Wanting It Badly Isn't Enough

Video 14 · Read It Once · narration script (draft 1)
Sources, credited in the description and not spoken: Think and Grow Rich (Napoleon Hill)

## Hook

Pip has wanted to start his own business for years.

He thinks about it on the bus. He watches videos about it at night. Every new year, he promises himself that this is the year.

Five years later, he is still in the same job, still wanting the same thing, just as badly as before.

Pip doesn't lack desire. He has plenty of it. In this video, you'll learn why wanting something badly isn't enough, and the few things that turn a wish into something that actually happens.

## A wish is not a plan

Almost everyone wants more. More money, a better job, a healthier body, a business of their own.

But most of that wanting stays a wish. It's vague, it has no date, and it asks nothing of you today.

The difference between a wish and a goal you reach isn't how strongly you feel it. It's how clearly you have defined it, and what you are willing to do about it.

## Step one: make it exact

The first step is to turn the wish into something exact.

Not more money, but a specific amount. Not someday, but a specific date. Not my own business, but what you will sell, and to whom.

A vague desire gives your mind nothing to work on. An exact one gives it a target. And once there is a target, you start noticing the people, ideas, and chances that could get you there.

## Step two: decide what you'll give

The second step is to decide what you will give in return.

Nothing comes for free. Every result costs something. Time, comfort, money, evenings, or the safety of the familiar.

Most people skip this part. They want the result, but they never decide on the price. So when the price shows up, they back away.

## Step three: a plan you start now

The third step is a plan, and starting it before you feel ready.

The plan doesn't have to be perfect. In fact, the first plan is usually wrong. What matters is that it exists, so you have something to act on and something to improve.

Waiting for the perfect moment is just another way of staying in the wishing stage.

## Step four: keep it in front of you

The fourth step is to keep the goal in front of you, every day.

Write it down. Read it in the morning and again at night. It sounds simple, maybe even silly, but it keeps the goal from fading back into a wish while daily life gets busy.

## Step five: persistence

And the final step is persistence.

Plans will fail. Some of them will fail more than once. People who reach big goals don't avoid failure. They treat a failed plan as a sign to change the plan, not to give up the goal.

Desire starts the engine. Persistence is what keeps it running.

## Close

Let's go back to Pip.

He writes down one exact goal. Ten paying customers for a small design service, by the end of June. He decides to give up two evenings a week to get there. He makes a rough plan and starts it that same week.

His first plan fails, so he changes it. By July, he has twelve customers.

He didn't want it more than before. He just stopped wishing and started deciding.

So here's what to remember. A wish is vague, a goal is exact. Every result has a price, so decide what you'll give. And the people who get what they want aren't the ones who want it most. They're the ones who don't stop.
