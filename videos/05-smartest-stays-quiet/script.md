# Why the Smartest Person in the Room Stays Quiet

## Hook

Pip is selling his old car. He's hoping for four thousand dollars, and a buyer has come to look at it.

The buyer walks around the car slowly, and says almost nothing.

So Pip fills the silence. He mentions the new tyres. Then the small scratch on the door. Then that he needs the money by Friday. Then, still met with silence, he offers to knock off five hundred dollars.

The buyer never asked for a discount. He never made a single argument. He just stayed quiet, and let Pip talk himself down.

In this video, you'll learn why silence has so much power over people, how to spot when it's being used on you, and how to protect yourself by saying less.

## Why silence works on us

Here's what happened inside Pip's head.

Most people find silence in a conversation deeply uncomfortable. A few seconds of quiet can feel like a minute. It feels like something has gone wrong, and that it's our job to fix it.

So we fill the gap. And when we fill it, we rarely fill it with our strongest points. We fill it with doubts, extra details, apologies, and offers we never planned to make.

The quiet person doesn't have to push. They just have to wait. The nervous person does the work for them.

## The more you say, the more you give away

There's an old idea about power that says the more you talk, the more likely you are to say something foolish.

Every extra sentence is information. When Pip mentioned that he needed the money by Friday, he handed the buyer the most valuable fact in the whole conversation. Now the buyer knew that time was on his side.

And every extra explanation can sound like weakness. When someone over-explains a price, a decision, or a request, it often sounds as though they don't quite believe it themselves.

People who speak less don't seem to know less. They often seem to know more. Their few words carry more weight, and others start leaning in to work out what they're thinking.

## How to spot it

Silence is not always a tactic. Sometimes people are just thinking. But here are three signs that quiet is being used to move you.

Sign one. The silence comes right after you've named a price, an offer, or a decision. It lands exactly at the moment where your nerves are highest.

Sign two. The other person asks a short, open question, and then says nothing at all while you answer, and keep answering.

Sign three. You notice that you're the one making all the moves. You're adding details, softening your position, and making offers, while the other person has given you almost nothing in return.

When you see these signs, it doesn't mean the other person is a villain. It just means you're negotiating against yourself.

## How to protect yourself

So how do you stay steady when the room goes quiet?

The first move is to say your piece, and then stop. State your price, your answer, or your request, once and clearly. Then let the silence belong to the other person.

The second move is to count. When a pause feels unbearable, count slowly to five in your head before you add anything. Most of the time, the other person speaks first.

The third move is to decide what you won't share before the conversation starts. Your deadline, your lowest price, and your reasons are yours to keep unless sharing them clearly helps you.

And the fourth move is simply to get comfortable with quiet. It isn't rude, and it isn't awkward. It's just space. The person who is relaxed in that space is usually the person in control.

## Close

Let's go back to Pip.

A month later, Pip sells his old laptop. He names his price, and then he stops talking. The buyer looks at it for a long time. Pip counts slowly in his head, and says nothing.

The buyer pays the full price.

Pip didn't become a master negotiator overnight. He just learned that he didn't need to fill every silence.

So here's what to remember. Silence makes most people talk, and talking gives things away. When the room goes quiet right after a big moment, notice who is doing all the work. And when you've said what you need to say, stop, and let the quiet do its job.
