# How Manipulators Gain Power Over You (and How to Spot It)

Video 12 · Read It Once · narration script (draft 1)
Sources, credited in the description and not spoken: The 48 Laws of Power (Robert Greene)

---

## Hook

Pip starts a new job, and one colleague is wonderful to him from day one.

She praises his work in every meeting. She shares little secrets about the office. She treats him like the one person in the office she really trusts.

Six months later, Pip is doing half of her work, he has stopped talking to the rest of the team, and he feels anxious every time she goes quiet.

Pip never decided any of this. It happened one small step at a time. In this video, you'll learn the most common moves manipulators use to gain power over people, and how to spot them before they work on you.

## Move one: too much, too soon

The first move is warmth that arrives too fast.

Big praise, early trust, and special attention make you feel chosen. And when you feel chosen, you lower your guard.

Kindness itself isn't a warning sign. The warning sign is intensity that doesn't match how well the person actually knows you.

## Move two: making you dependent

The second move is to make themselves necessary.

A manipulator wants to become the person you go to for information, for approval, or for opportunities. The more you depend on them, the more they can ask of you.

Watch for anyone who quietly becomes your only source of something important.

## Move three: cutting you off

The third move is isolation.

It rarely looks like an order to stay away from people. It looks like small comments. Hints that others don't respect you. Little stories that make your friends or teammates seem untrustworthy.

Over time, you drift away from the people who would have noticed what was happening.

## Move four: hot and cold

The fourth move is unpredictability.

Warm one day, distant the next. Generous one week, cold the following week, with no clear reason.

This keeps you off balance. You start working harder to win back the warm version, and your attention is always on them.

## Move five: the small favour that grows

The fifth move is the slow ask.

It starts with something tiny that is easy to say yes to. Then the next request is a little bigger. Each step feels reasonable compared to the one before, so you never notice how far you've travelled.

## How to protect yourself

So how do you protect yourself without becoming paranoid?

The first move is to watch actions over time, not words in the moment. Anyone can be charming for a week. Patterns take months to fake.

The second move is to keep your circle wide. Stay close to several people who know you well. They will notice changes in you that you can't see yourself.

The third move is to notice how you feel. If someone regularly leaves you anxious, guilty, or confused, that feeling is information.

And the fourth move is to practise small refusals. Saying no to something minor shows you, and them, where the line is.

## Close

Let's go back to Pip.

He starts having lunch with the rest of the team again. He notices that the stories about them don't match what he sees. He turns down one extra task, and then another.

The colleague's warmth disappears almost overnight. And that tells Pip everything he needed to know.

So here's what to remember. Too much warmth too soon is a signal. Anyone who makes you dependent or cuts you off is gaining power. And the best protection is a wide circle, and the confidence to say no.
