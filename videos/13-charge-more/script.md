# Why Charging More Can Get You More Customers

## Hook

Pip makes custom websites for small businesses. He charges less than anyone else in town, because he thinks that's how you win customers.

He works twelve hour days. His clients haggle over every invoice, ask for endless changes, and pay late. And at the end of the month, there's barely anything left.

Then a friend suggests something that sounds crazy. Tripling his price.

It sounds like the fastest way to lose every customer. But in this video, you'll learn why charging more can bring you better customers, better results, and more money, and why being the cheapest is usually a trap.

## The race to the bottom

When you compete on price, you're in a race where the winner is whoever is willing to earn the least.

There will always be someone cheaper. So you end up working harder for less, with no money left to improve what you sell.

And low prices send a message. To many buyers, cheap means risky. They wonder what's wrong with it.

## Price changes the customer

Here's the part most people miss. Your price chooses your customers.

Customers who pick the cheapest option often care only about price. They tend to be the most demanding, the least loyal, and the first to leave when someone cheaper shows up.

Customers who pay more have usually decided the problem really matters to them. They take it seriously, follow advice, and value the result.

So a higher price doesn't just bring in more money per customer. It often brings in better customers.

## Price changes the result

There's a second effect, and it's even more surprising.

People who pay more tend to get better results. When something costs a lot, people commit. They show up, they put in effort, they make it work. The price itself makes them pay attention.

And with more money from each sale, you can afford to deliver more. More time per client, better tools, extra support. The product actually gets better because the price went up.

## The good cycle

Put it together and you get a cycle.

A higher price brings committed customers. Committed customers get better results. Better results bring stronger reviews and more referrals. And more demand lets you raise your price again.

The cheap path runs the same cycle in reverse. Low prices, stretched time, average results, and customers who leave.

## How to charge more

So how do you actually do it?

The first move is to make your offer clearly better, not just more expensive. Add the things your best customers value most, like speed, guarantees, or extra support.

The second move is to talk about the result, not the work. A business doesn't want a website. It wants more customers. Price against the value of that.

The third move is to raise prices on new customers first. Watch what happens, then adjust.

And the fourth move is to accept that some people will say no. That's the point. You're making room for the right ones.

## Close

Let's go back to Pip.

He adds a fast delivery promise and three months of support, and he triples his price. A few old clients leave. But the new ones say yes faster, ask fewer questions, and pay on time.

He works fewer hours, earns more, and his websites are better than ever, because he finally has time to do them properly.

So here's what to remember. Being the cheapest is a race nobody wins. Your price chooses your customers. And a higher price, backed by a better offer, can make everything better, for you and for them.
